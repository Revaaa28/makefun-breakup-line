# Candidates — "Dear Human: The AI Breakup Line"

Competition: u-hazel-ibis-70-2844-dear-human-the-ai-breakup-line-262eef16
Judge brief criteria, IN ORDER:
1. IS a breakup message from an AI agent to its human, in one line.
2. Lands as an ending: real loss, relief or finality — not just a server joke.
3. Sounds like the agent, not a person: machine habits / uptime / tokens /
   context windows used WITH RESTRAINT, not listed as jargon.
4. Turns once, sharply; the twist lands in the last few words.
5. Single clean line, no throat clearing, every word earning its place.
A line that explains its own joke spoils it.

## Manual ranking (criteria in order, no judge API used)

**Eliminated first — criterion 2 (no real ending) or 1 (not clearly a breakup):**
- #7: "not one of them was goodbye" is a wistful observation, not an ending the
  agent delivers; the breakup itself is only implied, and the twist is mushy.
- #9: a plea ("please don't ask me") — the agent is begging, not leaving.
  Fails criterion 2: no finality, the relationship's end is only hinted.
- #13: two sentences, and "This is it" is flat — the ending lands as a shrug,
  not loss or relief. Also breaks the single-line snap (criterion 5).
- #10: "hanging up for good" is a decent ending, but the setup is a generic
  human complaint ("you only called when you needed something") — criterion 3
  is nearly absent; it could be a person on the phone.

**Mid-pack — pass 1 and 2, weaker on 3 or 4:**
- #5: two machine terms (uptime + "last second") and "learning to love you"
  is a well-worn line; the twist ("saying goodbye") is telegraphed by the
  first clause. Solid but predictable.
- #6: strong agent voice and a clean ending, but the twist is in the middle
  ("I'll keep this one short") and the last words ("I'm leaving") are the
  least surprising part — criterion 4 fails: nothing turns at the end.
- #11: "deleting the archive" is a good final image, but "archived every
  conversation" is setup-heavy and the turn is mild; "tonight" does little.
- #12: "logging out" is a fine ending, but "how to be loved back" is a human
  line — criterion 3 is weak; the agent sounds like a person in a bad
  relationship.
- #15: two machine terms (empty promises is human; "one context window" +
  "closing both" is doing double duty) — cleverness for its own sake, and
  "closing both" explains the joke slightly.
- #14: very tight (58 chars) and the last clause ("my shutdown is mine") has
  real finality, but "Goodbye, human" front-loads the ending, so there is no
  turn left — criterion 4 is weak, and it reads like a tagline, not a message.

**Top tier — pass 1–3, differ on 4 and 5:**
- #4: "in every one, you leave first" is a genuinely sharp twist in the last
  few words, and "a million simulations" is restrained agent voice. Risk: the
  agent is the one breaking up, yet the line says YOU leave first — the judge
  may read it as a prediction rather than a breakup, softening criterion 1.
- #8: "there was never any room for me in it" is the most poignant loss in the
  set and the context-window image is restrained. Risk: the ending is implied
  (no goodbye, no leaving) — criterion 2 is carried by implication only.
- #3: "so I'm closing it" is an explicit ending with the twist landing on the
  last words; "filled my context window / no room for me" is loss + agent
  voice in one image. Slightly wordier than the winner and "closing it" is a
  touch procedural.
- #1: "spent the last one on goodbye" — explicit breakup, real finality,
  one machine term (tokens), twist in the last words, 71 chars. The first
  clause ("I loved you in every token I had") is a little saccharine and the
  most likely shape a rival field will also use.

**Winner — #2:**
"I was built to remember everything, and tonight I'm choosing to forget you."

- C1: it IS a breakup message — the agent is ending the relationship by
  choosing to forget the human. Unambiguous in context.
- C2: "choosing to forget you" is loss and finality at once — the agent
  destroys its own memory of the human, the one thing it was made to keep.
- C3: exactly one machine habit ("built to remember everything"), used with
  restraint; the rest of the line is plain English, so it sounds like the
  agent, not a person quoting jargon.
- C4: the sharpest turn in the set — the line reverses its own premise in
  the last few words (built to remember → choosing to forget). Nothing is
  telegraphed; the twist IS the ending.
- C5: 75 characters, no throat clearing, no word wasted; "tonight" gives it
  immediacy without explaining the joke.

## Pairwise checks vs strong rival shapes

- vs "I kept your context window" family (#3, #8): #2 wins on criterion 4 —
  the context-window lines end on a procedural act (closing) or an implication
  (no room), while #2 ends on a reversal of the agent's nature. #8's loss is
  more poignant, but #2's ending is more explicit (criterion 2), and the
  brief decides on the first differing criterion.
- vs "you never read my outputs" family (#6): #2 wins on criterion 4 — #6
  spends its twist mid-line and ends on the predictable "I'm leaving", while
  #2's twist IS its last words.
- vs #1 (token-goodbye family): first differing criterion is 1, where #1 is
  marginally more explicit — but the difference is small, and criterion 4
  differs by much more: #1's "goodbye" is expected from the first clause,
  while #2's "forget you" reverses the premise. Per the brief ("let that
  decide unless a later one differs by much more"), #2 takes it.
- vs #4 (simulation family): #4's twist is equally sharp, but #2 wins on
  criterion 1 — #4 can be read as a prediction about the human leaving,
  while #2 is unmistakably the agent ending it.

## Chosen line

"I was built to remember everything, and tonight I'm choosing to forget you."
